# Research

Done 2026-11-19 from public sources. Re-check before relying on anything time-sensitive.

## The user

A registered nurse on **5 St. John, Hackensack University Medical Center** (Hackensack, NJ),
full-time nights. Cross-training in the CTICU, not transferring.

### 5 St. John
- HUMC job postings list it as "Unit Based Telemetry/Stepdown", also tagged
  "Med/Surg-Tele Overflow". FT and PT postings are the same unit.
- Telemetry / stepdown (also called PCU): every patient on continuous cardiac monitoring,
  more acute than a regular floor, less than ICU, typically about 3 to 4 patients per nurse.
- Public postings do not say what kinds of patients the unit mostly gets. Not confirmed
  as cardiac-specific.

### CTICU
- Cardiothoracic ICU: patients right after open-heart surgery (bypass, valves), on support
  devices (IABP, Impella), ventilators, chest tubes, arterial and PA lines, pressors, and
  temporary epicardial pacing wires.
- Patients who improve often step down to a unit like 5 St. John, some still with pacing
  wires and at risk of post-op atrial fibrillation (common pattern at most hospitals;
  not confirmed for HUMC). This is the overlap the app's CTICU section targets.

### The EKG exam
- A hospital rhythm-strip interpretation test given on hire to tele / ICU nurses, often a
  vendor test (for example Relias) or an internal one. Separate from CTICU training.
- Common pass marks: 80% (Upstate), 85% (Banner), 90% reported for a Relias basic test.
  Many require 100% on lethal rhythms (VT, VF, asystole, torsades) regardless of overall
  score. Strips are usually 6-second lead II.
- He has passed it at several hospitals, so basic rhythm ID alone will bore him. The app
  has to go past it.
- Update 2026-11-19: he is retaking a dysrhythmia exam during orientation. He knows the
  rhythms; test anxiety is the problem. Provider unconfirmed (HUMC internal "Dysrhythmia
  Challenge", HealthStream, Relias/Prophecy or other), so the app teaches general
  interpretation and practice under a clock, not a copy of any exam.

## Similar apps (where this one can be better)

| App | What it does | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| SkillStat Six Second ECG | 27 lead II rhythms, random, timed "name it" game | Recognition only. No "what now", no tracking of misses |
| Practical Clinical Skills | 650+ strips, multiple-choice drills with feedback, graded quizzes | Big library, no stepdown actions, no post-op pacing, desktop-feel |
| Arrhythmia (iOS) | 23 lead II rhythms, side-by-side study mode | Small set, no exam mode, no context |

This app's edges:
1. **"What now" scenarios**: strip plus vitals, then stable vs unstable and the next action
   on a stepdown unit. None of the three do this.
2. **Post-cardiac-surgery strips**: epicardial pacing modes, failure to capture / sense,
   post-op AFib. Not in any of the three nurse drills.
3. **Weak spots come back**: missed rhythms resurface more often (spaced repetition).
4. **Built for nights**: dark, low-glare, offline, one-handed, 5-minute sessions.
5. **Measuring on the strip**: calipers and a 6-second rate count, the skill the exam tests.
6. **Endless strips**: generated by code, so no memorizing the same 20 pictures.
